01 · CENTRAL TENDENCY

📊 Mean

Mean is the average value of a set of numbers. It is calculated by adding all values and dividing the total by the number of values.

Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values
Example

Data: 10, 20, 30

Mean = (10 + 20 + 30) / 3 = 20

🎤 Interview Question

What is mean?

Mean is the arithmetic average of a set of numerical values. It is calculated by dividing the sum of all values by the number of values.
02 · CENTRAL TENDENCY

📍 Median

Median is the middle value of a dataset after arranging the values in ascending or descending order.

Example

Data: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50

Median = 30

If there are an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.

💡 Interview Tip

Median is often useful when the dataset contains extreme values or outliers because it is less affected by them than the mean.

03 · CENTRAL TENDENCY

🔁 Mode

Mode is the value that occurs most frequently in a dataset.

Example

Data: 2, 4, 4, 5, 6

Mode = 4

Because 4 occurs more frequently than the other values.

🎤 Interview Question

Can a dataset have more than one mode?

Yes. A dataset can have two or more values occurring with the same highest frequency. Such a dataset can be described as multimodal.
04 · PROBABILITY

🎲 Probability Basics

Probability measures how likely an event is to happen. Its value ranges from 0 to 1.

Probability = Favorable Outcomes / Total Outcomes
Example

A coin has two possible outcomes: Head and Tail.

Probability of getting Head = 1 / 2 = 0.5

🎤 Interview Question

What does probability 0 mean?

Probability 0 means the event is impossible under the given conditions.
05 · DISPERSION

📐 Variance & Standard Deviation

Variance measures how far data values are spread from the mean.

Standard deviation is the square root of variance and represents the spread of values in the same units as the original data.

Simple Understanding

Low standard deviation → Values are close to the mean.

High standard deviation → Values are more widely spread.

🎤 Interview Question

What does standard deviation tell us?

Standard deviation tells us how much the values in a dataset typically vary around the mean.
06 · RELATIONSHIPS

🔗 Correlation

Correlation measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables.

Types

Positive Correlation: Both variables tend to increase together.

Negative Correlation: One variable tends to increase when the other decreases.

Zero or Weak Correlation: Little or no linear relationship.

🎤 Interview Question

Does correlation always mean causation?

No. Correlation shows that two variables are associated, but it does not by itself prove that one variable causes the other.
07 · DATA QUALITY

🚨 Outliers

An outlier is a data value that is unusually far from the other observations in a dataset.

Example

Data: 10, 11, 12, 13, 100

Here, 100 is much larger than the other observations and may be considered an outlier.

How can we detect outliers?
  • IQR method
  • Box plot
  • Z-score
  • Domain knowledge
🎤 Interview Question

Should every outlier be removed?

No. First we should investigate why the outlier exists. It may be a data-entry error, or it may represent a genuine and important observation.
08 · INTERVIEW

🎤 Statistics Interview Questions

QUESTION 01

What is the difference between mean and median?

Mean is the average of all values, while median is the middle value after arranging the data. Median is generally less affected by extreme values.
QUESTION 02

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance measures the average squared deviation from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of variance and is expressed in the same units as the original data.
QUESTION 03

What is correlation?

Correlation measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables.
QUESTION 04

What is an outlier?

An outlier is an observation that is unusually far from the rest of the data.
QUESTION 05

Why is statistics important for a Data Analyst?

Statistics helps Data Analysts summarize data, understand variation, identify patterns, compare groups and make data-driven decisions.

💡 Statistics Interview Tip

Don't memorize formulas alone. First understand what the statistical concept means, then explain it using a small real-world example.
